# Resources

The Energy Finance Tracker database is a project of Oil Change International’s Public Finance Program, which is working to end public finance for fossil fuels and shift it to support a just energy transition. You can read our latest program updates and press releases [on our website](https://oilchange.org/). Below you can find direct links to key public finance research from Oil Change International and our partners.

## Research Spotlight

 [Public Enemies](https://priceofoil.org/?p=43627) (2024) is the most recent analysis of our G20 international public finance dataset from Oil Change International and Friends of the Earth US. This report provides an update to previous reports: [At A Crossroads](http://priceofoil.org/g20-at-a-crossroads) (September 2022), [Past Last Call](https://priceofoil.org/2021/10/28/past-last-call-g20-public-finance-institutions-are-still-bankrolling-fossil-fuels/) (2021), [Still Digging](http://priceofoil.org/2020/05/27/g20-still-digging/) (2020) and [Talk is Cheap](http://priceofoil.org/2017/07/05/g20-financing-climate-disaster/) (2017) detailing earlier trends.

 [Private Fantasies, Public Realities: Why private finance isn’t delivering an energy transition and the case for public sector leadership](https://oilchange.org/publications/limits-to-private-finance-approach/) (2025) details how governments’ dominant “private-sector first” approach to financing a just energy transition is prolonging the fossil fuel era. A just energy transition is affordable and secure but it hinges on public finance and planning.

 Several studies suggest that carbon capture and storage (CCS) and fossil hydrogen do not necessarily advance the energy transition. [Carbon Capture’s Publicly Funded Failure](https://oilchange.org/publications/ccs-data/) shows that billions in subsidies have been pledged to these projects, many of which involve using captured carbon dioxide to enhance oil recovery. Additionally, [Bubble Burst: Why Norway’s Blue Hydrogen Fantasy is Over Before It Started](https://oilchange.org/publications/bubble-burst-why-norways-blue-hydrogen-fantasy-is-over-before-it-started/) demonstrates that fossil-based hydrogen is economically and environmentally unsound.

## Recommendations for domestic public finance institutions

National Development Banks (NDBs) – publicly-owned institutions that can invest in the public good – have underused potential for financing a just energy transition. Some NDBs are already providing clean energy finance, but could provide far more. Some are still providing significant fossil fuel financing.

## Energy Access

The UN Sustainable Development Goals call for universal access to affordable, reliable, and modern energy services by 2030, but a lack of finance means we are not on track to reach this target. The following reports look deeper into the Public Finance for Energy Database to show that very little G20 public finance is flowing to energy access of any kind, and that the distributed renewable energy that is most needed to close the gap is particularly under funded.

- [African Energy Leadership Report: The Case for 100% Renewable Energy](https://www.powershiftafrica.org/publications/african-energy-leadership-report) (2025)

## Financial Architecture Reform 

Global financial architecture (the set of rules governing financial flows) has been in the spotlight in most major international political forums in the past few years as more and more policymakers and analysts recognize that it is a major driver of growing economic inequality, unfair sovereign debt crises, and climate disasters. Global South-led movements and governments have proposed several reforms that have momentum behind them. The resources below provide key background on these proposals. The proposed reforms would help create an economic system that would support rather than hinder fair public funding for a just transition to a renewable economy, addressing climate impacts and critical social services. 

- [We Can Pay for it](https://oilchange.org/publications/we-can-pay-for-it/) (2025)
- [Reclaiming tax sovereignty to transform global climate finance](https://taxjustice.net/reports/reclaiming-tax-sovereignty-to-transform-global-climate-finance/) (2025) 
- [A Coherent Framework for Sovereign Debt and Economic Transformation: Towards Global South Debtors’ Coalition](https://iej.org.za/sovereign-debt-towards-a-global-south-debtors-club/) (2025)
- [Finding Finance: Tax Justice and the Climate Crisis](https://www.actionaidusa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Finding-the-Finance-Tax-Justice-and-the-Climate-Crisis-6th-June.pdf) (2024) 
- [Adapting our Financial Architecture in a Crisis-Prone World: A Civil Society Proposal for Special - Drawing Rights Reform](https://www.actionaidusa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/CSO-SDRs-Reform-FINAL.pdf) (2024) 
- [The Debt-Fossil Fuel Trap: Why debt is a barrier to fossil fuel phase-out and what we can do about it](https://debtjustice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Debt-fossil-fuel-trap-report-2023.pdf) (2023) 

## CCS and fossil hydrogen

CCS and fossil hydrogen are often presented as solutions for reducing carbon dioxide emissions. However, several studies suggest that these technologies can prolong reliance on fossil fuels, raising questions about their effectiveness in promoting the energy transition.
